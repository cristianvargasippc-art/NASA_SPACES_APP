01 // NASA SPACE APPS CHALLENGE

Why Space Mission Design Is a Balancing Act

Designing a space mission requires balancing competing demands. In InterNautics: Orbit Lab, every major design choice has a direct consequence, bridging abstract engineering concepts into tangible gameplay.

Interdependent Variables

Adding a powerful scientific instrument increases data and scientific value, but also hikes mass, power consumption, cost, and downlink requirements.

No Single Perfect Solution

Different combinations of spacecraft, instruments, orbits, and launch vehicles can achieve the same mission objective, encouraging creative problem-solving.

Causal Feedback

Instead of simply stating "Mission Failed", the game explains *why* (e.g., battery drain during eclipse or insufficient ground antenna bandwidth).

03 // MULTIPLAYER CONCURRENT ENGINEERING

4 Specialized Roles, One Shared Mission

Play solo or form a team of up to 4 participants where each member manages competing responsibilities and must negotiate collectively before launch.

ROLE 01

Mission Director

Defines strategic objectives, oversees global budget allocation, selects target orbit, and authorizes launch windows.

PRIORITY: Budget & Compliance
ROLE 02

Spacecraft Engineer

Designs structure, solar arrays, power systems, and ADCS, verifying mass limits against launch vehicle capacities.

PRIORITIES: Mass & Power
ROLE 03

Science Lead

Selects scientific instruments (cameras, spectrometers, radars) to maximize scientific return and discovery value.

PRIORITIES: Scientific Value
ROLE 04

Comms Officer

Manages onboard memory buffer, downlink antennas, and ground station contact windows to prevent data loss.

PRIORITIES: Downlink & Storage
